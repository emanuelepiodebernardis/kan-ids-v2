Exact additive score decomposition makes additive Kolmogorov–Arnold networks suitable for direct arithmetic inspection. We use IoT intrusion detection as a case study of integer export verification and microcontroller implementation. Exhaustive enumeration of finite-domain edge functions includes integer rounding; signed error intervals give sufficient agreement certificates relative to the frozen integer coefficient evaluator. A 20,554-byte table certifies 42,206 of 42,209 prepared test decisions, with empirical agreement on all decisions; the coefficient arrays occupy 254 bytes. The certificates cover coefficient-to-LUT agreement only: export from the original floating model changes 40 test decisions and is not certified. Under a controlled cycle-counter protocol with Flash code and DRAM parameters, the LUT is about 1.71 times faster on ESP32-C3, whereas it is 7.58% slower under the original five-implementation protocol. A separate sustained workload cycles the same 500 source flows used by the hardware comparison and records whole-board USB energy and batch time under one boundary. Coefficient-to-LUT energy estimates decrease from 498 to 92.2 µJ per iteration on Mega 2560 and from 0.987 to 0.859 µJ on ESP32-C3. Instrumented runs report observed static, stack and heap components, without claiming an exact whole-system memory peak. An endpoint-pair-disjoint floating-model experiment gives additive-KAN balanced accuracy 0.8948 and false-positive rate 20.59%, both worse than those of the evaluated GAM and MLP baselines. These results support an auditable implementation study while showing the detection limitations of the chosen additive model.
