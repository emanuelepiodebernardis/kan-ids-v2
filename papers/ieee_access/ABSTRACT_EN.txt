We study integer coefficient and sampled lookup-table representations of a frozen additive Kolmogorov–Arnold network for intrusion detection. The coefficient arrays occupy 254 bytes and the selected table 20,554 bytes. Exhaustive enumeration of each edge's finite Q12 domain includes the implemented integer rounding. Signed edge-error extrema give sufficient certificates for threshold agreement, certifying 42,206 of 42,209 prepared test decisions; all decisions agree empirically. A smaller 10,314-byte table leaves twelve decisions uncertified, compared with three for the selected table. This retrospective comparison retains the training-only fallback selection and does not establish a minimum-memory solution. Physical 500-flow kernel experiments give a 5.746-fold LUT speedup on Arduino Mega 2560. A controlled ESP32-C3 factorial experiment gives a 1.706-fold speedup with Flash code and DRAM parameters and identifies a code–parameter placement interaction. A separate USB-energy pilot uses five implementations, two acquisitions per implementation and board, and repeated batches of twenty prepared source flows. Whole-board energy estimates for coefficient and LUT calls are 492.62 and 79.64 microjoules on Mega and 0.8747 and 0.5516 microjoules on ESP32-C3. These descriptive estimates use measured central-window power and MCU batch duration; they exclude preprocessing and do not resolve individual calls. Retrospective cross-dataset stress tests show poor performance. Source-to-cache checks and reconstructed training cohorts identify changes in category–label associations without establishing their contribution to historical errors. The contribution is an auditable agreement analysis and measured memory–latency–energy trade-off, not universal detection superiority or unconditional lossless compilation. Peak runtime memory remains unmeasured.
