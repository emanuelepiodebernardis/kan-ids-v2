We examine whether a sampled lookup table preserves the decisions of a frozen additive integer Kolmogorov--Arnold network and what implementation cost this representation change entails on two microcontrollers. Exhaustive enumeration includes the exact rounding of each finite-domain edge function; signed error intervals give sufficient agreement certificates relative to the integer coefficient evaluator. A 20,554-byte table certifies 42,206 of 42,209 prepared test decisions, with empirical agreement on all decisions; the coefficient arrays occupy 254 bytes. A controlled ESP32-C3 experiment compares representation, code placement and parameter placement under a common timing boundary. With code in Flash and parameters in DRAM, the LUT is about 1.71 times faster. Separate physical USB measurements support whole-board engineering estimates for a sustained workload that repeatedly cycles twenty prepared inputs; estimated energy per call decreases by about 84% on Mega 2560 and 37% on ESP32-C3. The experiments exclude live preprocessing and do not measure peak runtime memory. Historical classification results and duplicate-sensitivity analysis delimit the evaluated detector rather than establish architectural superiority. The contribution is an auditable integer-export analysis and physical implementation case study, with explicit limits on generalization across workloads and networks.
